Licensing

Free software under the AGPL-3.0, or a commercial licence that replaces it. The code and the models are the same.

The AGPL, free

Use it, study it, change it, share it. If you distribute a product built on it, or let people use one over a network, publish that product's source under the same licence. Testing it and measuring it on your own data asks nothing of you.

The commercial licence

For a product whose code stays closed: a server, a mobile app, a web platform. It covers the code and the shipped models, every language, for one product and one year, with no limit on volume. Prices, terms.

What no licence of ours covers

Nothing OpenPronounce imports is under a copyleft licence. Three things around it are, and keep their own licence:

  • espeak-ng (GPL-3), needed for every language but English. It runs as a separate program: OpenPronounce reads what it prints without linking any of it. If you ship espeak-ng, its licence applies to it alone.
  • The Piper voices (GPL-3), an optional extra for offline reference readings.
  • The mouth drawings of the web demo.

Older versions

Releases 0.1 to 0.4 were published under the MIT licence and stay under it.
