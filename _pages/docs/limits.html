What it does not do

These limits were measured; the figures come with them. Read them before showing scores to your users.

It learned from one population

Non-native speakers of English from a single corpus, half of them children, reading sentences of 2 to 12 words. The scores follow their raters: a clean adult reading gets about 80, not 100, and an accent from elsewhere is less well covered. Measure what that costs on your own speakers.

Children are scored less well

The speech models were trained on adults. With the sentence, the score follows the raters at 0.85 for adults and 0.67 for children of 12 and under. Without it, 0.82 and 0.42. Do not use free speech with children.

Free speech was measured on read sentences

No rated corpus of spontaneous speech can be used. The free-speech figures come from read sentences with their text hidden. On 22-second recordings of adults, the scores follow the raters at about 0.75. Without the text, words are reported as unclear, never as mispronounced.

Hesitation lowers the score, even for natives

A pause or a restart is a fault in a read sentence, and that is what the scorer learned. Natives talking freely on the radio score 66 to 67 where natives reading score 72. Expect the same for any fluent speaker who thinks while speaking.

Fast native speech is not rewarded

The sound recognizer is less sure of fast speech, and the scorer takes that for a lack of clarity. A careful non-native speaker can outscore a native.

A missing word is found more surely than an extra one

83 in 100 words left out are reported as not said, and 0.7 in 100 words really read are reported by mistake. Speech in excess is located 63 times in 100. A single extra sound, "an developer" for "a developer", goes unnoticed.

A phone line moves the score

Through a simulated land line (8 kHz, narrow band), the scores still follow the raters: 0.78 against 0.81 with the sentence, 0.64 against 0.68 without. But one recording's score moves by 3 to 4 points on average, and a real call adds noise that was not simulated. Do not compare a score taken on the phone with one taken on a microphone.

Long sentences are outside what it learned

The scorer never saw more than 10 words. Natives get 77 on sentences of up to 12 words, 73 from 20 words up.

Only English was measured against raters

The six other languages are checked on native speakers, not on rated non-native speech. See Languages.

It is lenient where the raters are

Function words, word-final consonants, accent traits the raters would not mark: it lets them pass too. The sound recognizer itself mishears about one sound in ten on a clean native reading. A word missing from the English dictionary is pronounced by rule, marked guessed, and never blamed.

A bad recording is scored anyway

Too quiet, clipped, noisy or too short: audio_quality.issues says so and reliable turns false. Showing the score or not is your call.
